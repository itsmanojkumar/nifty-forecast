For Week 1 I cleaned and preprocessed a real financial dataset: the official NSE daily closing values of the Nifty Metal index from November 2015 to October 2026, 2698 trading days in total. Using Python, NumPy and Pandas, I loaded the data, converted dates to a datetime index, and checked for missing values, duplicates and gaps. There were no missing values or duplicates, and the largest gap was 5 days, which is just weekends and holidays. I then engineered features: daily log return, a 20-day moving average, 20-day volatility and 60-day momentum. I found 13 outlier days using z-scores, and chose to winsorise them at the 0.1% and 99.9% quantiles instead of deleting them, because they are real market shocks. Categorical variables, namely the weekday and a high or low volatility regime, were one-hot encoded with drop_first to avoid the dummy variable trap. Numeric features were normalised with StandardScaler and MinMaxScaler, and I verified the resulting means, standard deviations and ranges. The final dataset has 2638 rows after removing rows lost to rolling windows. In the exploratory analysis the index shows an annualised return of 19.1% with annualised volatility of 27.9%, and daily moves ranging from -11.6% to 9.8%. Weekday averages differed slightly, but I treat that as noise. The cleaned data and code feed my Nifty sector forecasting project, and each step is documented in the attached report.
